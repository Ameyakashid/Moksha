# Dead Cells melee combo/charge evidence → next MOKSHA PC pass

Read-only study, 2026-10-04. Scope: one original chained melee action for the PC, integrated with movement, shield and passive fixtures. No NPCs, new level, imported assets, copied implementation, game execution or runtime edits.

**Result:** Dead Cells clearly separates weapon cycle, anticipation, input release, charge, animation completion, cooldown and owner permissions. Several concrete weapon bodies preserve useful dispatch/cleanup rules. The public source does **not** recover a complete combo scheduler, a fresh-press queue, per-hit timings or a general cancel matrix. The implementable next step is an explicitly authored MOKSHA chain with separate step definitions and input/lock/strike ownership, not a claimed v35 port.

## Provenance and confidence

- Pinned source: [N3rdL0rd/DeadCellsDecomp, `77c3ad263c4187ade6d048ba77bb61f79e6cec44`](https://github.com/N3rdL0rd/DeadCellsDecomp/tree/77c3ad263c4187ade6d048ba77bb61f79e6cec44), identified by its README as PC v35 / build `50edff4`. The prior movement, combat and shield audits remain applicable.
- **BODY:** inspected nonempty implementation; **CALL:** real dispatch into missing behavior; **DECL:** fields/signatures/stubs; **PRIMARY:** historical official developer evidence; **MOKSHA:** proposed original policy. A body is not independently proven runtime equivalence. Numeric IDs are left uninterpreted.
- Source-file identities and bounded facts are recorded in [MELEE_COMBO_SOURCE_FACTS.json](MELEE_COMBO_SOURCE_FACTS.json). No recovered gameplay timing values or copied source are included.
- The complete pinned tree lacks `BaseMelee.hx`. `WeaponSkill` is present as a secondary class in [Weapon 275–285][weapon], and inherits **OldSkill**, not the newer `Skill` class. Do not transfer the latter's area/event scheduler into weapons without evidence of a caller.
- Targeted GitHub searches found only missing consumers for `anticipateNext`, `attackAnimDone`, `holdToAttack`, `tryToCancelCharge`, `needButtonRelease` and `requireRelease`. Early-combo/early-charge overrides also remain stubs. These searches corroborate the inspected gaps; they do not prove the absence of all dynamic calls.
- No controlled gameplay timing capture was measured. Official notes establish qualitative rules, and the artist's article establishes animation intent; neither supplies exact v35 clip timelines, hit frames or cancel windows.

## Primary developer constraints and their limits

- [Update 1, 2017-05-31][p1] explicitly allows an active skill such as a grenade to cancel a Broadsword attack. It does not specify a v35 phase table or extend that permission to every action. Its fixes for unwanted Dual Daggers cliff launches and shooting-assisted mega-jumps make traversal regressions useful tests.
- [21, December 2020][p21] describes Katana's normal slash followed by a held charged dash. [23][p23] fixes loading that charge while a shield is held. These establish a distinct held-charge branch and cross-weapon arbitration; they do not authorize concurrent shield-and-charge for a generic MOKSHA weapon.
- [23.6, 2021-03-25][p23] lengthens Oven Axe's combo window without giving its value. The launch notes describe a repeatable final hit. This is weapon-specific follow-up behavior, not proof that all chains wrap, require contact or keep the same step on repeat.
- [33.1 and 33.3, March 2023][p33] restore Katana cancellation and its air technique. A fix preserving a named technique is not a universal instant-cancel rule. [35.4][p35] fixes reverse-direction rolling while holding Morning Star; weapon-facing and roll-facing must not be accidentally conflated.
- [Thomas Vasseur's 2018 animation article][artist] describes establishing strong, correctly timed attack key poses first, with interpolation before/after them and effects reinforcing impact. Timing revisions also change poses. This supports distinct original anticipation/contact/follow-through silhouettes for each MOKSHA step; it supplies no v35 frame counts or permission windows.

Avoid three misleading uses of “combo”: the weapon's ordered attack cycle, a hit-streak damage buff, and the named Combo mutation are different concepts. For example, [34][p34] gives the mutation a 2.5-second window; that is **not** the sword's next-press buffer or chain-reset duration. Likewise the broader movement audit's kill-triggered run-speed combo is not a melee cycle.

## Bounded original MOKSHA implementation contract

**Recommended scope:** one ordinary authored chain with distinguishable steps; a three-step slash/slash/finisher is a reasonable MOKSHA choice, not a recovered Rusty Sword/Broadsword count. Reuse the existing PC and passive damage/prop fixtures. A charged finisher, weapon family, resource cost, target-seeking movement and lens/progression system are separate features; expose seams without adding them implicitly.

1. **Step profile:** each step owns windup/active/recovery, permitted next step, queue acceptance/commit window, facing policy, ground/air motion policy, contact shape/offset, damage/impulse and original pose keys. A separate chain profile owns idle retention/reset and optional hold-repeat. Every numeric value and permission row is authored MOKSHA tuning.
2. **Input intent:** represent fresh edges with a sequence/identity separate from current held state. Use one pending next-step request for the initial implementation, consuming that edge only once. A request does not execute while admission is denied; define expiry and whether a rejected transition preserves it. Prevent a blocked request from becoming an unexpected delayed attack after unrelated traversal or hurt. Single-slot capacity and all retention rules are MOKSHA choices.
3. **Step commit:** validate chain identity, expected step, action generation, external permissions, shield permission/lock and cancellation before starting. Atomically retire the old strike, choose the next step, increment the attack serial, capture facing and clear only the new strike's hit set. Do not clear shield recovery or unrelated locks to satisfy an attack. User callbacks may reset/change action during events; guard against a stale continuation after every externally observable dispatch.
4. **Queue versus charge:** an accepted fresh follow-up never becomes a held charge merely because the button remains down. If optional hold-repeat is included, synthesize its request only at a defined admissible boundary; it neither floods a queue nor bypasses cooldown. Hold-to-repeat and hold-to-charge require a weapon-specific priority if both ever exist. The simple chain can omit charged behavior entirely while keeping its interface distinct.
5. **Interruption:** retain an explicit per-step/phase table for shield, roll, jump, air jump, dive/drop, climb/wall/ledge, hurt/stun, forced action and reset. Commit admission before mutation; denied transitions leave the old step intact. Chain cancellation/retention policy is separate from ending the current hitbox. Preserve M8's shield cancellation gate, locks and reentrancy checks. Never interpret a permitted locomotion transition as permission to erase post-use locks.
6. **Contacts and presentation:** each committed step gets a new strike identity; one target can be hit once by that step under the existing PC policy and again by a later accepted step. Confirmed contact drives hitpause/noise/impulse once, with current occlusion and moving-carrier safety. Rebuild sweep history at the new step's origin. Author three readable pose sequences instead of playing one animation faster or only increasing damage.
7. **Air and speed:** initially preserve the tested PC's airborne/fall-risk behavior unless the pass explicitly defines a change. Any target-conditioned air assistance needs its own policy and passive fixture proving eligibility; a breakable prop is not automatically the “enemy in strike zone” in the historical note. If attack speed becomes configurable, explicitly define which clocks scale and verify timing boundaries. Do not derive hover, refreshed jumps or universal invulnerability from weapon use.

The inspected M8 work-in-progress PC still has one generic windup/active/recovery attack, a fresh-press buffer, serial/target set, per-phase permissions, shield control locks and generation checks. Relevant seams are `_try_attack`, `_advance_combat`, `_cancel_attack`, `_weapon_allows_cancel`, `_refresh_weapon_permissions`, `strike_snapshot`, `confirm_strike` and input-edge capture. Snapshot SHA-256 at inspection: `69b74005a5e2bae8e89287e1c089795de910bc4ebfcace2ecf880aad418cc7e4`; M8 work was ongoing, so re-read the stable handoff before implementing. This audit did not alter that file.

MOKSHA's [original spec](../../docs/MOKSHA-source.md), lines 15–20 and 255–259, calls for fast side-on combat, physical objects reacting to attacks, impactful sound and tiny contact pauses. Lines 97–119 reserve later independent speed/power and parry tradeoffs. These goals support data-driven step timing and impact strength; they do not require a stamina system or progression in this pass.

## Passive-fixture acceptance matrix

| Case | Required check for the declared MOKSHA policy |
|---|---|
| One edge versus held input | One fresh press produces one step with repeat off. Holding cannot replay that edge. Release/re-press inside the acceptance window queues exactly one follow-up |
| Queue boundaries | Too early, first accepted tick, last accepted tick, too late, repeated presses, release during hitpause and simultaneous attack/shield/roll. Expose input sequence, queue reason and consumption; no invisible backlog |
| Full chain and reset | Step order/finisher and pose identity are visible. Idle expiry returns to the configured start; attack cancellation, hurt, reset and forced state follow explicit retention rules. A stale queued finisher never fires in a later chain |
| Hit/miss/occlusion | Continue on hit and whiff according to the authored rule. Same target once per step, again on next step; two targets each once; wall-occluded target never hit; stale previous-step confirmations rejected |
| Facing/reversal | Mirror each step left/right. Turning at the permitted boundary changes the next attack only; active-step target geometry/impulse remain committed. A roll may choose its permitted direction without mutating the old weapon hit |
| Ground/air/carriers | Each step from grounded, rising, apex/falling and landing; slopes, low roof, ledge, moving support and wall proximity. No collision bypass, extra jump, fall-risk reset, unintended hover, carrier sweep or old hitbox after cancel |
| Cross-action permissions | Every phase versus shield/roll/jump/drop/dive/climb/wall/ledge and external restrictions. Accepted transition cleans one active strike; rejection preserves it. Shield lock survives melee queue/hold-repeat; concurrent shield-charge is not silently allowed |
| Hitpause and reentrancy | New input survives the intended local pause once. Pause cannot advance multiple steps, resample the old strike across carrier travel or multiply contacts. Event callback reset/hurt/permission change aborts stale next-step commits |
| Time scaling and recovery | Boundary tests at ordinary and altered time steps/speed, if supported; no skipped contact phase, unintended overlap or timer reset through action spam. Recovery, combo retention and cooldown remain independently observable |
| Feedback and regression | Each step has a distinct original contact pose, follow-through and appropriate sound/noise/impulse. No-contact/duplicate/blocked transitions create no fake hit feedback. Existing M8 shield, movement, physics-prop and traversal checks remain intact |

The unresolved v35 values are: queue depth/window, input edge consumption, stage timing/count for the reference weapon, retention/reset conditions, early-chain permission, held-repeat/charge priority, phase-by-phase turn/move/cancel rules, target-conditioned air math, strike geometry/lifetime/deduplication, cast-speed scaling, cooldown/control lock, critical conditions and exact animation events. Passing the above validates MOKSHA's documented contract, not numerical Dead Cells parity.
